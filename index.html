Kerro Privacy Policy

Effective November 25, 2026

In short: Kerro keeps your workouts, food log, weight, habits, and progress photos on your iPhone. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. Kerro sends food searches and barcodes to two public food databases so it can look up nutrition information. If you choose to turn it on, Kerro also keeps a backup copy in your own iCloud Drive.

Who we are

Kerro is an iPhone app made by Stevenson Studios. Questions about this policy can be sent to crusher.asps4m@icloud.com.

What Kerro stores on your iPhone

Everything you enter or create in Kerro is stored in the app's private storage on your device. That includes:

  • Your profile (such as name, age, height, and goals) if you enter them
  • Workouts, programs, and exercise history
  • Food and nutrition logs, saved foods, and meals
  • Weight entries and habit tracking
  • Progress photos you choose to add, which are resized and saved inside the app
  • App settings

We do not have access to this data. It does not leave your device unless you choose to back it up, export it, or share it, as described below. Deleting the app, or using Settings → Reset All App Data, removes it from your iPhone.

Apple Health

If you connect Apple Health, Kerro can read your step count, workouts, and weight, and can save Kerro workouts and weigh-ins to Health. Each type of access is optional and you control it in Kerro and in the Health app. Health data is used only inside the app to show your progress. It is not shared with us or anyone else, and it is never used for advertising.

Food search and barcode scanning

Kerro includes a built-in database of everyday foods that is searched on your phone with nothing sent anywhere. For packaged and brand-name foods, the food name you search for, or the barcode you scan, is sent to these public services so they can return nutrition information:

  • USDA FoodData Central (api.nal.usda.gov)
  • Open Food Facts (openfoodfacts.org)

These requests do not include your name, profile, workouts, weight, Health data, or photos. As with any internet request, those services can see your IP address, and their own privacy policies apply to what they receive. Kerro also remembers recent search results on your phone so repeat searches are fast.

Food data: USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts (© Open Food Facts contributors, available under the Open Database License).

Camera

Kerro uses the camera only when you scan a food barcode. Images from the camera are processed on your device to read the barcode and are not saved or uploaded.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Kerro schedules reminders (such as a weight reminder) on your device. These are created locally and do not involve a server.

Backups and iCloud

Kerro lets you create a backup file of your data. A backup goes only where you send it, for example to the Files app or another app you choose.

You can also turn on iCloud backup, which is off by default. When it is on, Kerro keeps a copy of your data (optionally including progress photos) in your own iCloud Drive and refreshes it about once a day. This copy is stored in your personal iCloud account and is handled by Apple under Apple's terms. We cannot see it. You can turn it off at any time and delete the backup files from the Files app.

Anyone who has a backup file can read what is in it, so keep it somewhere you trust.

What we do not do

  • We do not require an account.
  • We do not sell or share your personal information.
  • We do not use advertising, analytics, or tracking tools.
  • We do not collect your data on our own servers.

Children

Kerro is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Health and safety

Kerro provides general fitness and nutrition information. It is not medical advice. Talk to a qualified professional before changing your exercise or eating habits, and stop exercising if you feel pain or become unwell. Nutrition values come from public databases and can differ from a product's label.

Changes to this policy

If we change how Kerro handles data, we will update this page and its effective date, and the app's "How Kerro Uses Data" screen will reflect it.
